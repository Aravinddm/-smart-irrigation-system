🌱 What is Soil Moisture?

Soil moisture is the amount of water held within the spaces between soil particles. It's usually measured as a percentage, representing how saturated the soil is compared to its maximum water-holding capacity.

Why It Matters

Soil moisture directly affects plant growth, crop yield, and ecosystem health. Too little water stresses plants and stunts growth; too much can drown roots and promote disease.

Factors Affecting Soil Moisture

Soil Type: Sandy soils drain quickly and hold less water; clay soils retain water longer but drain poorly.

Weather: Rainfall, humidity, and temperature all directly change how much moisture is present and how fast it evaporates.

Irrigation: Manual or automated watering directly adds moisture, and scheduling affects how consistently levels are maintained.

Drainage: Slope, soil compaction, and underlying rock layers affect how quickly excess water leaves the area.
